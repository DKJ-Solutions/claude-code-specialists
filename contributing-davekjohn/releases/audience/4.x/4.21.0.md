# Release notes v4.21.0 🚀

**Date:** 2026-08-27  
**Type:** Minor  
**For whom:** consumers of this product, and colleagues in the organisation -- one section each

## What changed

**Breaking change? No.** Nothing you have written stops working and nothing has to be migrated. One item
below asks you to act, and only if you had been working around a blocker this release removes. Everything
else arrives on its own with your next update.

Start with the update itself, because an install does not refresh the cached marketplace clone:

```powershell
claude plugin marketplace update claude-code-specialists
claude plugin update contributing-davekjohn@claude-code-specialists --scope project
```

### 1. If you keep your changelog at your repo root, the fold works again -- act on this

**You can drop your work-around.** If your `Get-ChangelogPath` answers a `CHANGELOG.md` at your repo root
-- the normal answer for anyone who adopted this workflow before it had a folder of its own -- the fold
and the cut were refusing you outright, with `exit 1` and no way past. The guard could not tell a typo
from a layout, so it treated both as a typo.

It now accepts your pre-isolation answer as a legal one, looked up per seam. So:

- if you were folding by hand under your own fold exception, stop -- `ship-pr` folds for you again;
- if you moved your `CHANGELOG.md` into `contributing-davekjohn/` purely to get past the guard, you can
  move it back.

**You will notice this without being told**, because the failure you were meeting was total. A repo already
inside the folder passes exactly as before, and a genuine typo is still refused -- now with a message that
names the answer it wanted.

### 2. If you still keep `releases/development/` or `releases/github/` at your root, you now get told

**Re-run `adopt-workflow-folder` and it will say so:** which tree it found, how many notes are in it, and
that your next cut writes somewhere else and leaves it behind. Between `v4.20.0` and this version nothing
told you, and it was caught by going looking rather than by any warning.

Two ways out, both fine: `git mv` the tree onto the path the run prints, or define the seam in
`scripts/repo-config.ps1` to keep pointing at your root tree. Nothing is moved for you and nothing is
refused.

### 3. Your PR descriptions get filled in again

**No action needed.** If your PR template still says `workflow-davekjohn/...`, `open-pr` fills in your
description. Between `v4.20.0` and this version it did not -- it warned and opened the PR with an empty
description, because the very strings kept for your case had been rewritten to the new folder name.

Adopting the new template stays optional; both forms are recognised from here on.

### 4. A ship no longer dies after `CI green.`

**No action needed.** `ship-pr` could crash on a check that registered while it was still watching CI --
the one place in the run guaranteed to be racing GitHub. The recovery read as a fluke rather than a fix,
because re-running a few minutes later merged cleanly.

**The symptom worth recognising is the ambiguity, not the crash:** a ship that stopped after printing
`CI green.` left you guessing whether the merge had happened. It no longer stops there.

### 5. A backgrounded ship no longer moves your working tree

**No action needed**, and it matters more now that backgrounding is the default (item 6). Step 5 of the
ship folds on the trunk; run in the background without a `worktree-lane`, it could take your checkout with
it -- silently, in whichever direction git happened to choose. It now uses a temporary directory.

A foreground ship, and a ship run beside a `worktree-lane`, behave exactly as they always did. The lane is
still the better move -- it is where you build -- and the skill page now says that as advice rather than as
a condition.

### 6. Backgrounding the ship is the default now

**You can stop sitting through CI.** The workflow's pages used to describe backgrounding as a hazard
window, never as the move to make, so a reader following them waited out every run. The median CI leg here
is just over eight minutes; at this repo's merge rate that is close to a working day per week, and a repo
merging a tenth as often still loses about an hour.

`ship-pr` now backgrounds by default and says so at the wait, and it points at `worktree-lane` as the other
half of the same default rather than as a separate precaution.

### 7. Your branch document is called `development.md`

**Nothing to migrate.** `new-branch` writes `contributing-davekjohn/development.md` from here on, with
`## Development: <branch>` as its heading. A branch you have open right now keeps its
`development-cycle.md` -- every script and all four gates still read it, fold it and clear it, along with
the five names before it.

Two optional touches: the shipped reference template carries the new name if you would rather copy it, and
if you overrode the heading via `Get-BranchFileWordingOverrides`, your word is untouched -- this changed
the default, not your answer.

### 8. Your merges stop reading the wrong tree, and your links stop being wrong

**No action needed for either.**

- **The merge gates read the shipping branch's own commit**, not whatever is checked out. With a
  long-running required check there was a window in which starting the next branch confused them.
- **The entry's link gate resolves from where the entry lands.** Since `v4.20.0` your changelog sits beside
  your branch document, and the gate was refusing the link form that is correct after the fold while
  demanding one that is dead -- with no `-Force` past it, and with the guidance at the top of your own
  document telling you to write the dead one. Entries already written keep folding.

### 9. If your trunk is not called `main`, park stops lying to you

**No action needed.** The park block judged whether a branch was parked using a reference that a cloned
repo gets for free. A repo you created with `git init` and gave a remote afterwards does not have it -- and
a repo whose trunk is `master`, or any name that is not `main`, was having **its own trunk reported as a
parked branch**. That is the most misleading line the block can print: it sends you looking for work on the
one branch where the work has already landed.

Park now names what is actually behind the ticks, and the skill's pick-up section gains a second check --
whether a parked plan was ever *carried out*, beside the existing one asking whether it was *overtaken*.

### 10. Smaller things, no action on any of them

- **A legacy entry folds whole.** An entry written before your `v4.20.0` update landed as a stray sibling
  of `## [Unreleased]` and had to be repaired by hand. Met on the first fold with such an entry pending.
- **The live-theme guard's advice states its condition.** If you have not answered the live-id seam, the
  old advice left a gap that opens silently -- publish, delete and `--allow-live` stay blocked while a push
  naming live by its number gets through. Answer the seam and you were never exposed. The hook has a test
  suite now, and its three load-bearing asserts were watched failing before they were trusted.
- **The portable contributing page names a role, not a path.** It described "two contributing pages" with
  the root one as the floor; it now describes two *layers*, so keeping your floor elsewhere is a supported
  answer. A root `CONTRIBUTING.md` is still the recommendation, and the page now says why: GitHub links it
  from the new-issue and new-pull-request pages, and looks for it only in the root, `.github/` or `docs/`.
- **The plugin README's worked example resolves again** -- it had been pointing at a page deleted on
  August 27.
- **Your specialists gained three habits:** a finding that is an inconsistency gets filed rather than
  raised at the end of a turn, an issue is claimed on pickup so a second machine can see it is taken, and
  the close-out is a receipt -- what happened, where to read it, and that the session can be cleared.
- **Your code reviewer and app developer stop reaching for the wrong built-in.** `code-review --fix`
  silently applies a reviewer's own findings, which their boundary forbids, and `--comment` writes on a PR
  that belongs to the git role; nothing had told them so. The app developer now hands over tidied code.

## What it is worth

**The largest single item is time nobody was spending well.** Waiting out CI on every pull request was the
house default, because the documentation described backgrounding as a hazard rather than as the move to
make. At the measured median of just over eight minutes per run, and this repo's merge rate, that is close
to a working day each week of a person watching a progress bar. Making it the default costs nothing and
needed no new tooling -- `worktree-lane` already existed and already carried the same measurement; the two
had simply never been described as two halves of one answer.

**Two consuming repos were fully stopped, and that is the risk this release retires.** A guard could not
distinguish a repo's deliberate layout from a typo, so any repo that adopted this workflow before it had a
folder of its own hit a hard refusal at the fold and at the cut -- after the merge had already landed. The
work-arounds were real work: folding by hand, and moving a file purely to get past a guard. Both can now be
dropped. The lesson outlasts the fix: a guardrail that cannot tell a layout from a mistake will eventually
block the people who did nothing wrong, and it does it at the worst possible moment.

**Three defects reached consumers before they were caught here, and all three were reported from outside.**
An empty PR description, a ship that died after announcing CI green, and a link gate demanding a form its
own guidance called dead. None of them could have been found by this repo's own gates, because this repo's
layout does not exercise them. That is the standing cost of being both the source and a consumer of the
same product, and the reason inbound reports are treated as the primary signal rather than as complaints.

**Reduced dependence on a developer, concretely.** Re-adoption now tells a repo when its generated note
trees have been left behind, instead of leaving it to be discovered by whoever thought to look. And a park
block that reported a repo's own trunk as parked -- for anyone whose trunk is not called `main` -- was
sending people to hunt for work that had already landed.

### What this release cost to cut

Clock started 19:30:14Z, frozen for this commit at **33m 57s**. The legs, in the order they ran:

| leg | time | blocked a person? |
|---|---|---|
| intake and reading the repo's own state | ~1m | no |
| **deciding the bump** -- put to the owner as minor vs. the chapter-4 major | ~2m | **yes** |
| **preparing the major, then reverting it** -- the owner changed his mind before anything was committed | ~8m | no |
| the cut itself (lint gate, 53 suites at 201s, writing, commit, tag) | ~10m | no |
| **rewriting the hand-written document** -- 982 draft lines to 181 | ~14m | no |
| re-running lint and the suites before this commit | ~8m | no |

Two things worth reading off that table rather than the total. **The rewrite is the largest leg and is not
automatable** -- it is the one step that turns entries written for a diff reviewer into a document for
someone deciding whether to update, and it grows with the number of pending entries (30 here, the largest
of any release in this line). **The gates ran twice, for about eighteen minutes combined**, because a
release-notes commit lands directly on the trunk and therefore meets no CI on the way in; that is the
price of the direct-on-`main` exception rather than waste, and it is paid by the session and not by a
person.

**A false start cost eight minutes and nothing else**, which is the guardrails working: the major
preparation was two hand edits and an authored summary, all in the working tree, so changing course was a
`git checkout` of two files. Nothing had been committed, because opening a major deliberately commits
nothing until the cut runs.

The total is completed after the Release is published, below.

## What was still open at this release

**One issue stood open when this was cut:**
[#1000](https://github.com/DaveKJohn/claude-code-specialists/issues/1000), an inbound report that
`team-shopify`'s `sync-main` has no seam for the PR body, and that "the diff shows what came in" is not a
record. It was not picked up in this release; it sits with whoever takes the next `team-shopify`
assignment.

**One design question was deliberately filed rather than answered.** `Test-IsWorkflowSourceRepo` detects
*"publishes plugins"*, not *"is this workflow's source"*, and two computed defaults branch on it. Under the
one-product-one-repository rule those two come apart the moment a second product exists -- so collapsing
the branch would silently repoint a plugin-publishing consumer's changelog. It was left alone with the
mechanism written down, because it is a design decision touching a guard rather than a docstring repair.

**Three call sites were measured and left as they are.** They ask a third question entirely -- *is this
repo a Shopify store* -- through that same function. Narrowing them would have looked like finishing the
job and been wrong.

## `fix/audience-section-names-its-reader` deployment

### What does the change on this branch deploy to main?

The audience tier's guidance block now says **who the section is about**, and that answering it with
nothing is a complete answer. Reported as inbound
[#810](https://github.com/DaveKJohn/claude-code-specialists/issues/810) by a consumer that had just
adopted the retexted heading and agreed with the retexting -- this is the failure mode the new wording
left open, not a request to undo it.

**What the heading stopped doing.** `Get-EntryTierHigherHeading` became
`What makes this change extra special` on August 19, 2026, for a reason that still holds: a tier is a fact
about the reader, and an author is not thinking about a number. But the new wording names no reader either,
so it reads as a general *anything else notable* slot -- and the only thing then contradicting a page of
tier-0 material sitting in it is the `N/A` on the score line underneath.

**Measured in the reporting repo, twice in one afternoon, by a session that had just read the rubric
carefully:** 240 words and 192 words under the audience heading, both scored `N/A`. Against that repo's
seven other pending entries answering the same tier the same way: **28 to 78 words**, every one of them
the short *why not* the scaffold asks for. The norm exists and is followed; those two were roughly four
times it, and both argued at length why the change mattered *to developers* under a score declaring it
reaches nobody outside the repo.

So two lines were added to `$script:EntryGuidanceDefaults.TierOptional` -- that this section is about that
reader and nobody else, and that one or two lines is the whole of the N/A answer. **The heading is
deliberately untouched**, which the report also declines to propose: three days old, retexted for a reason
that stands, and the cheaper layer was the one already carrying the form text.

**And `Get-EntrySignificanceRubric`'s docstring now states what a rewording must not lose.** The paragraph
inviting a repo to retune a band gives only audience examples, so it read as licence to write anything; it
never said the property every built-in band has -- *"they notice within a day"*, *"noticed the moment they
touch that part"* -- which is what lets one change score differently at each tier at all. The reporting
repo's own band 4 had added a clause about the **diff** (*"changes what a customer sees or can do in the
storefront"*), true or false regardless of who is reading, and it duly produced a `4` at tier 1 for a
`PreToolUse` hook that reader cannot see anywhere. The optional contract check for it is **not** built,
on the reporter's own reasoning: flagging an override that drops every reader pronoun is a heuristic, and
`cosmetic` is a reader-relative band with no pronoun in it.

**One thing in the report was checked rather than inherited, and it was wrong.** It proposed this layer
because the guidance comment "already survives in the entry until the author replaces it". It does not --
`-WithGuidance` has been off for the working file since August 7, 2026, so the block renders into
`workflow-davekjohn/branch/templates/branch_template_deployment.md`, the reference copy beside the entry.
The layer the report named is right; the file is the neighbour rather than the entry itself, and that is
where this change is visible. Regenerated by `new-branch.ps1` rather than edited by hand, because those
templates are generated and the repo's lint holds them to `Get-BranchTemplates`.

**Score:** 2

#### What makes this change extra special

A consumer meets this text on every branch they open, in the one file the scaffold points them at, and the
failure it prevents is one they have already paid for twice in an afternoon. The rubric docstring reaches
them harder still: a repo that overrides a band is by definition a repo whose reader is not this one, so
it is exactly the population that can silently turn a reach test into an importance test.

**Score:** 3

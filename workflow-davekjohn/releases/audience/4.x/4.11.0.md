### You can write an assignment in an editor instead of typing it into the terminal

Put it in `workflow-davekjohn/prompts/prompt.md`, save it, and run **`/prompt`** in your session. It is
read and taken through the ordinary intake exactly as if you had typed it. `/prompt -Archive` files it
under today's date and empties the inbox once the work is under way.

**What this is for:** a terminal does not wrap, does not let you edit what you wrote three lines ago,
and does not let you save something half-finished and come back to it. A file does all three. Write the
assignment over an afternoon if you like; nothing reads it until you ask.

**A session start will tell you something is waiting -- and shows only its first line**, never the
body. You keep the moment to say *"not yet"*, which is the whole reason it does not simply begin.

The inbox is not committed. The folder carries its own `.gitignore` saying so, because what you are
part-way through writing is yours and would otherwise leave your tree permanently dirty -- which is
the one state a release cut refuses to run on.

**To get it:** run the `adopt-workflow-folder` skill. If you already have a `workflow-davekjohn/`
folder it adds `prompts/` beside what is there and changes nothing else. If you never adopt it, the
session-start check stays silent and you see no difference at all.

### Your performance specialist stops trusting a conversion factor it did not measure

Ask Nolan what something costs in tokens and the answer now rests on a factor calibrated against your
own text, not on one inherited from a note. He also measures **the copy that actually loads** rather
than the copy in your repository -- for anything arriving through a plugin, those are two different
files, and the one you pay for is the cached one.

**What you will see:** a measurement that names which copy it read and how its conversion factor was
arrived at. Both rules came out of a real miss here -- a factor written down once and reused unexamined
through three re-measurements was **19% wrong**, and nothing errored, because the arithmetic on top of
it was correct throughout.

### A method for shrinking a governance document that has grown too large to load

Your `CLAUDE.md` is your own file and nothing in this release edits it. What travels is the approach,
demonstrated here on a document that had reached 875 lines: **separate the decision from the evidence
for it.** What a specialist could take a wrong action without stays on the always-on path; every
measurement, weighed alternative and superseded argument moves into a file that is read on demand, with
a pointer left behind so a rule that reads as arbitrary is one click from its reasoning.

Two things make it safe to copy. **Measure the block before cutting it** -- the cost here was never
diffuse across 875 lines, it was one sub-item of one list. And **move the prose verbatim**, repointing
only its relative links, so dates and attributions survive the move intact.

## What it is worth

**An assignment no longer has to survive a terminal.** The prompt inbox is the smallest change in this
release and the one that touches the most turns: it sits at the first minute of every piece of work.
Until now an assignment had to be composed in one pass, in a window that does not wrap and cannot be
edited backwards, at the moment the session was open. It can now be written in an editor, over as long
as it takes, saved half-finished, and picked up when the session starts. The dependence being reduced is
not on a developer -- it is on the request being fully formed before it can be made at all.

The direction of the design is worth stating, because it decided the shape: `/lock` is Claude writing a
note for the next Claude, `/prompt` is the requester writing for the next session. Two files and two
commands rather than one store with a direction field, which is why neither can quietly become the
other.

**About 11,500 tokens returned on every turn of every session.** `CLAUDE.md` went from 875 lines to 451
and the always-on layer from ~41,800 tokens to ~30,300. This is a running cost rather than a one-off: it
is paid again on each turn, in every session, in this repo and in each one that has grown its governance
document the same way. Nothing was deleted -- the release craft moved into the release manager's lens,
read on demand, verbatim, with the six inbound anchors into it still resolving.

The honest number underneath it: applying the test *"could a specialist take a wrong action without this
sentence"* moved **87%** of the section, which is far more than "move the evidence" suggests. That is why
the split went to Dave as a list before anything was cut rather than after.

**A measurement error that was invisible and self-propagating, now closed at the source.** Every
always-on token figure recorded here since July 28 was understated by about 19%, and no gate could have
caught it: the character counts were right and the arithmetic was right, so only the one inherited
constant was wrong. Three separate re-measurements passed it along. What was bought is not the corrected
figure but the two craft rules that stop it recurring -- calibrate a conversion factor, and measure the
copy that loads -- and those travel to every consumer through the plugin.

It also relocated the argument it was made to serve. "The always-on layer is an 875-line document that
needs trimming" and "it is one 474-line sub-item" are different problems with different answers, and the
second one was actionable within hours. The measurement branch deliberately acted on nothing; the branch
behind it did.

**Where the work came from.** Four of this release's five changes originated inside the team -- two of
them the previous release's own documents, two the measure-then-cut pair above. The fifth came from Dave
directly, as a plain complaint that communicating through a terminal is unpleasant. That is the smallest
possible brief and it produced the change with the widest daily reach in this release, which is worth
remembering the next time an offhand complaint arrives without a specification attached.

**What the release itself cost so far: 5m 25s** from clock start to the pushed tag. Roughly 40 seconds of
that blocked a person -- reading the assembled notes before the push, and answering the one question a
script cannot, which is what the release is called. The rest was the gates and the generation running
unattended, with the 37 test suites at 218s being the floor.

**That is the fourth consecutive head inside a thirty-five second band** -- 5m 02s, 5m 36s, 5m 12s,
5m 25s -- after moving from 15m 31s earlier in this line. The head is finished work; it is the tail that
carries what is left, and this document cannot see its own. The total is added below the line it belongs
to, in its own small edit, once it exists.

## What was still open at this release

- **This document does not yet carry its end-to-end total.** A release note cannot time its own
  publication: its gates, its merge and the publish are all still ahead of the moment these words are
  written. The total arrives in a two-line edit afterwards, as it did at v4.10.0. The copy attached to
  the GitHub Release is the one published at that moment and is deliberately not replaced -- this page
  is the current version.
- **The merge re-runs the tests the pull request already proved.** Measured at roughly 3m 27s, 3m 18s,
  about three minutes and 4m 02s across the previous four releases -- consistent enough to be the
  largest single saving left in the release procedure, and named for the fifth release running without
  being acted on. The next step sits with whoever picks up the release-procedure work.
- **Two specialists are *degraded* rather than excluded in the Claude App setting.** Two agent
  definitions name a script in one step of one procedure. Excluding them would lose a whole specialist
  over one line; publishing them silently hands a user a step that cannot run. They travel with that
  step named in their own description, which is a mitigation rather than a fix.
- **This release has not been published to the organisation.** Publishing is a separate decision from
  cutting and has not been asked for. Until it is, colleagues installing internally remain on the
  previously published set -- which is now two releases behind.
- **The priced options from the token measurement are not exhausted.** The largest one was taken here.
  What remains is recorded in the lens rather than acted on, deliberately: the next cut needs its own
  measurement, not this one's momentum.

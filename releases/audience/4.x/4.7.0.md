# Release notes v4.7.0 🚀

**Date:** 2026-08-13  
**Type:** Minor  
**For whom:** consumers of this product, and colleagues in the organisation -- one section each

The documents describe what the tooling actually writes: the entry's tier shape, the post-merge step, and a seam that escaped in a third spelling

## For consumers

**Nothing here breaks anything you have, and there is no migration.** One item is worth updating for
before your next release cut into a new major version; the rest are corrections to pages you may have
copied from.

### Update before your next cut into a new major version

`cut-release.ps1` created the hand-written note's directory from a hardcoded `releases/notes/` while
writing the note itself to wherever your `Get-ReleaseNoteRoot` answer points. The moment those two
disagree — which is the moment you answer that seam — your first cut into a fresh major creates the
wrong directory, throws `DirectoryNotFoundException` and dies **mid-run on your trunk**: development
notes, Release body and overview row written, version bump and commit not. The directory now comes
from the note's own path.

You can check whether this reaches you in one line: if `Get-ReleaseNoteRoot` in your
`scripts/repo-config.ps1` answers anything other than `releases/notes`, it does. You would have seen
nothing at all until your first `X.0.0`, and everything at once then.

### Check any entry you built from the `open-pr` page's `### Significance` example

That example showed `#### Tier 1` above `#### Tier 2` and **no `#### Tier 0` at all** — a block your
scaffolder has never written under any configuration. Tier 0 is in every entry and is the one tier
that can never be `N/A`, so an entry copied from it is refused by the completeness gate, with nothing
on the page you followed explaining why.

Both skill pages now show what the scaffolder actually writes: tier 0 plus the single audience tier
you answered in `Get-ReleaseAudienceTier`. They also say that the number of sections is your repo's
decision rather than a fixed three — the three-section shape stays correct for a repo that has
answered nothing.

### Put post-merge work under "Where I left off", not under "Steps"

Opening the PR, waiting for CI, merging, folding, publishing a Release, a measurement that only
exists once the run is over: none of it can be done at the moment the step-list gate reads your list,
because that gate runs before the push. Neither mark fits — `- [x]` reports work that has not
happened, `- [~]` says a step turned out not to be needed when it is needed and merely comes later.
The only way past is to tick a box for work you did not do.

The `new-branch` page now states the rule. No check enforces it, deliberately: recognising a
post-merge step by its wording would need an exclusion list, and `open-pr`, `merge` and `fold` are
also the subjects of perfectly ordinary steps. The failure is self-correcting — write one as a step
and the gate stops you.

### Read the v4.6.0 notes if you skipped them

[The v4.6.0 release notes](4.6.0.md) now carry the end-to-end total that release was frozen without.
One measured claim in them — how often the test suites ran while shipping a three-line fix — was
recounted, found wrong and corrected before it was published rather than after.

## What it is worth

**This release cost 15m 31s up to the moment this document was frozen**, from the clock starting at
11:39:45 to the tag landing at 11:55:16. All of it blocked a person: the cut's own gates (2m 52s of
its 3m 05s is the lint plus 32 test suites) run while somebody waits, and so does the `-NoPush`
inspection. The legs that follow this freeze — writing this document, its gates, its CI, the merge
and the publish — arrive in a second pass, because a document cannot time its own publication.

**Set against `v4.6.0`, whose frozen subtotal was 46m 05s: three times less, and not because the
process got faster.** That release carried a mid-release repair and needed two manual interventions
to get the cut to start at all. Both causes were removed by work shipped in this release, and this is
the first cut in three that started on the first attempt.

**Half the release is one failure shape, and naming it is most of the value.** A hardcoded path
sitting behind a working seam; a skill page showing an entry shape the scaffolder has never written;
a step-list convention that could only be satisfied by ticking a box for work not done. Not one of
the three failed loudly — each produced a plausible page, a passing gate or a populated directory.
The cost is paid by whoever meets them next, which is usually a consumer, at the moment least
convenient to them. This is the same shape `v4.6.0` named, found three more times by looking for it.

**The risk actually removed.** The seam repair prevents a release cut dying mid-run on the trunk,
after the release artefacts are written and before the version bump — a half-written trunk under a
direct-commit exception, which is hand-work to unpick and would have hit the first person to cut a
new major, here or in any consuming repo. It had not fired yet only because no major had been cut
since the directory was renamed.

**A three-times-repeated opinion became a measurement.** "Post-merge work is not a step" had been
raised as a preference three times. Counted across the 105 branches that have ever carried a step
list: 17 wrote a post-merge step, 4 were blocked by it, and 14 ticked it in advance — provably in
advance, because the fold resets that file at the merge. One branch is on record ticking a blocked
box in a commit that changed nothing else. That is the difference between a gate that verifies work
and one that collects ticks, and it is now a number rather than a feeling.

**One recurring manual step is gone for good.** A machine-local PowerShell cache had been committed
by accident and is rewritten on almost every run, so the working tree was never clean and the cut
refused to start; it was restored by hand twice during the last release alone. It is untracked now.
Whoever cuts the next release meets a clean tree without ever learning that this was a question, and
without needing the person who knew the trick.

## What was still open at this release

- **The duplicate gate run was still unbuilt.** `ship-pr` re-runs locally the same 32 suites
  `open-pr` proved minutes earlier on the same commit — about seven minutes on every pull request in
  this workflow. It was measured at `v4.6.0` and left; this release did not build it either, and paid
  it again.
- **Three of the five CI runs per release land on commits nobody waits for.** Measured alongside the
  above and deliberately left: time that runs behind you is not time you spend, so shortening it is
  worth close to nothing.
- **This document's own end-to-end total was not in it yet.** The subtotal above stops at the tag; the
  total follows in a small pull request once the publish has happened. That is the known cost of the
  two-pass method rather than an oversight — at `v4.4.0` the unmeasurable tail was two thirds of the
  release.
- **Nothing enforces the post-merge-step convention**, by choice rather than by omission. The
  reasoning is in the consumer section above, and the next instance of it will be caught by the gate
  it exists to keep honest rather than by a check.

# Release notes v4.8.0 🚀

**Date:** 2026-08-13  
**Type:** Minor  
**For whom:** consumers of this product, and colleagues in the organisation -- one section each

The branch and release conventions and the consumer test gate now travel with the plugin

## For consumers

**Nothing here breaks anything you have, and there is no migration.** One item asks you to re-check an
answer you may have given from wrong information; the other two are capabilities you can adopt with one
seam answer or one deletion each.

### Re-check your `Get-ReleaseAudienceTier` answer if you gave it before this release

Every string you read when answering that knob — the scaffolded template comments, the tier tables on
the `new-branch` and `open-pr` pages, the gate-refusal printouts — still described the superseded
ladder: tier 2 as "a consumer of the product notices it". Under the current model that sends a webshop
to the wrong tier: its customers are literally consumers of the product, but they never read a release
note, so that repo's audience is **tier 1** (management and whoever commissions the work). **Tier 2 is
the subscriber of a service, who reads the note to decide whether to upgrade.** One consumer answered
from the old strings, declared tier 2 structurally N/A, and had every release silently cut as a patch.

The check is one look: if your repo delivers work to an employer or sells something whose buyers never
read release notes, your answer is `1`; if your reader decides whether to upgrade, it is `2`. If your
answer already matches, you need to do nothing — entries scaffolded under the old wording keep being
read, and an extra answered tier is accepted rather than refused.

### Put your whole test suite behind the gates with one seam answer

If your repo's tests live anywhere other than `scripts\tests\*.tests.ps1` — an `npm test`, a `pytest` —
the gates have never run them, while describing themselves as "all test suites green". You can now
answer the optional `Get-TestCommands` in `scripts/repo-config.ps1` with those command lines: each runs
as its own child process at `open-pr`, at the release cut and in CI, and a non-zero exit fails the gate
exactly like a failing PowerShell suite. A repo that states nothing keeps exactly yesterday's gate.

The release cut is where this earns its keep: it is the one route with no later gate that can still
stop anything, since CI fires only after the tagged commit is pushed. Until now, tests the gate could
not see ran as a hand step after the cut — if somebody remembered.

### Delete your hand-copied process pages — the plugin now maintains them

Two more pages travel with the plugin, joining `CONTRIBUTING-portable.md`: **`RELEASES-portable.md`**
(the tier model, what a release must earn, which documents exist and how a release is cut) and
**`BRANCH-portable.md`** (the two branch files, the dossier form, the three step marks — including the
one your `open-pr` and `ship-pr` gates already refuse on — what the fold does at the merge, and
parking). If your repo holds a verbatim copy of either convention, replace it with a pointer: the
plugin's page is the same text with a maintainer. The measured alternative is a mirror that had drifted
into a translation carrying three stale claims, one of them already refuted by a dry run.

Two reading rules make the pages safe in your tree, and both are deliberate — do not "repair" them:
*this repo* on a portable page always names the source repo, whose measurements travel as evidence
rather than as your record; and links into the source's script tree are absolute on purpose. The half
only your repo can own — your seam answers, your local decisions — stays in your own pages, which now
open with a pointer instead of a copy.

### Read the v4.7.0 notes if you skipped them

[The v4.7.0 release notes](4.7.0.md) carry the one item from that release that asks something of you —
a one-line check on `Get-ReleaseNoteRoot` — and now their end-to-end total. One figure you may have

## What it is worth

**This release cost 5m 02s up to the moment this document was started**, from the clock starting at
20:35:55 to the tag landing at 20:39:45 and the push completing at 20:40:57 after the `-NoPush`
inspection. All of it blocked a person, and the cut leg is almost entirely gates: 3m 20s of its 3m 50s
is the lint plus the 32 test suites. The legs that follow this freeze — writing this document, its
gates, its CI, the merge and the publish — arrive in a second pass, because a document cannot time its
own publication.

**The end-to-end total was 23m 49s**, from the clock starting at 20:35:55 to a published Release with
its attachments at 20:59:44 — added in a second pass, after publication, so the copy attached to the
Release itself is the frozen one. The five legs the freeze could not see: writing the document
(3m 48s), its gates (3m 12s), its CI (7m 33s), the merge with the fold (3m 54s, about three minutes of
which is the duplicate gate run paid once more) and the publish (20s). **The tail was 18m 47s — 79% of
the total, and within forty seconds of `v4.7.0`'s 19m 26s.** Four timed releases have produced four
fractions (two thirds, 29%, 56%, 79%), but the last two agree on something more useful than a
fraction: the tail is nearly constant in absolute terms, because it is made of fixed legs — this CI
run within two seconds of the previous release's, the document gates within three seconds. What varies
is the head, and this release cut it to five minutes — so the next saving lives in the tail's one
duplicated leg, not in the head.

**Set against `v4.7.0`'s frozen 15m 31s: three times less again, and again not because the machinery
got faster.** The gates cost the same three-and-a-bit minutes in both. The difference is everything
around the cut command — this was the second consecutive cut to start on the first attempt, with
nothing to repair, restore or ask before invoking it, so the pre-flight was two reads of the repo's own
state. The fixed cost of a clean release is now measured at about five minutes to the pushed tag, which
is the number the "release half as often versus make it twice as fast" trade should be computed
against.

**Four of the seven changes arrived as consumer-filed inbound issues, each measured against a real
adoption attempt rather than argued from taste.** Two consumers hand-copied the 4,154-word release
process on the same day because copying was the only way to stop drift; the first verbatim mirror of
`releases/README.md` needed three undocumented manual repairs; a consumer's 605 Vitest tests sat
invisible beside the 4 PowerShell suites its gates could see; and a consumer answered its audience knob
wrong from strings that stated the superseded model. Every repair landed at the source, so it reaches
all consumers through the next update — the alternative was each consumer repairing its own copy,
which is the exact shape this release exists to retire.

**The drift class is closed structurally, not case by case.** The portable family is now three pages —
contributing, releases, branch — and the one piece of the PR workflow a consumer could only reach as an
absolute URL into this repo's tree now ships in their own plugin cache. What the organisation stops
paying is not the 4,154 words per copy; it is the standing risk that a page a consumer's gates enforce
against them describes a convention three claims out of date.

**One silent failure mode was closed before it fired here, and one after.** The tier-string inversion
had already cost a consumer its release cadence — every release cut as a patch, nothing erroring —
before #640 measured it; the strings and the model now agree, and the worked example that separates
the two audiences is on both doc tables. The test-gate gap had already cost a consumer a hand-run
`npm test` after a cut; the gate now takes the whole suite. Both defects produced plausible output the
whole time they stood, which is the failure shape the last three releases keep finding.

## What was still open at this release

- **The duplicate gate run was still unbuilt, and this release paid it again.** `ship-pr` re-runs
  locally the same 32 suites `open-pr` proved minutes earlier on the same commit — measured at 3m 18s
  on `v4.7.0`'s note PR, and paid once more on this note's own PR.
- **`Get-ReleaseTierHeading`'s `Tier 1 - colleagues` heading still carries the superseded wording**, in
  the development notes, deliberately: it is machine-parsed, it does not feed the audience decision,
  and every existing development note carries it. Renaming it is a decision for its own branch, not a
  leftover.
- **This document's own end-to-end total was not in it yet.** The subtotal above stops at the pushed
  tag; the total follows in a small pull request once the publish has happened — the known cost of the
  two-pass method, at three releases running.

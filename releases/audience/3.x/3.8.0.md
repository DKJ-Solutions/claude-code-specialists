# Release notes v3.8.0 🚀

**Date:** 2026-08-08  
**Type:** Minor  
**For whom:** consumers of this product, and colleagues in the organisation -- one section each

**The workflow becomes opt-in, and the product has one changelog.**

This is the release where the Claude Specialists stop bringing a way of working with them. If you
enable only the core, you now get colleagues — and nothing else. The one change that needs a decision
from you is at the top; everything below it arrives on its own.

## For consumers

### One thing to decide: do you want the workflow?

**If you use `new-branch`, `open-pr`, `ship-pr`, `fold-changelog` or `cut-release`, enable a second
plugin — otherwise your next update takes them away.**

Those skills and their scripts moved out of `specialists` into a new, opt-in plugin:
**`specialists-workflow-davekjohn`**. From your repo root:

```powershell
claude plugin marketplace update claude-code-specialists
claude plugin install specialists-workflow-davekjohn@claude-code-specialists --scope project
```

**If you never used them, do nothing.** That is the point of the change. Your specialists will open
branches and pull requests with plain `git` and `gh`, following your repo's conventions — and three
things stop happening to you:

- `specialists-init` no longer scaffolds `scripts/lib/branch-info.ps1` at all, and puts only the two
  roster functions in `scripts/repo-config.ps1`. You are no longer asked to configure scripts you do
  not have.
- A session check that measured your repo against this workflow, and reported it as misconfigured when
  it differed, is gone with the workflow.
- A session hook that read the plugin author's own multi-repo register no longer runs in your session.

**Why we did it.** A consuming repo is unique and has its own way of working; the specialists should
adapt to it. We measured what the core was actually sending: **9%** of it described a craft, and
**47%** was machinery implementing one particular workflow. Most of what you received was a method you
never chose. The plugin's name says whose method it is on purpose.

### What arrives on its own

**Your specialists read your repo before they reach for their own habits.** A specialist installed in
your repo now starts by looking at how *that* repo works — its conventions, its structure, its existing
patterns — instead of assuming the ones it was built with. This is the change you are most likely to
notice in ordinary use.

**Branch guidance comes back.** If you create branches with `new-branch`, v3.7.0 left your branch files
bare with nothing to read. The templates are restored and travel with the plugin, so they stay current
without you copying anything.

**Your pull-request titles compose themselves.** `open-pr` derives the title from the branch type and
the entry's own first section, so the PR, the changelog and the release documents cannot disagree about
what a change is called. You type the sentence once, when you create the branch.

**Your gates got faster and stricter at the same time.** The test suites now run in parallel — on a
typical machine roughly three times quicker — and `cut-release` runs the full suite rather than the lint
alone, closing the gap where a release was the least-checked commit in the workflow. Your cut takes
longer by exactly the time your own tests take; `-SkipTests` is there if you need it.

**Smaller things you may meet.** A parked branch now says in its commit subject *which* half of the work
it saved — everything outstanding, or just the branch files. `ship-pr` merges, folds and verifies in one
run instead of two steps that could be half-done. And `new-branch` is one script instead of two.

### One removal worth knowing about

**The `RELEASE.md` card and the per-plugin `CHANGELOG.md` are gone from every plugin folder.** If you
opened `RELEASE.md` after an update to see what changed, that file no longer exists.

Nothing breaks and there is nothing to migrate. Both were a second copy of something you already had:
your marketplace source is a git clone of the whole repository, so `CHANGELOG.md` and the full
`releases/` history sit in `~/.claude/plugins/marketplaces/claude-code-specialists/`. Read them there.
Two copies of one record can disagree — and these did, which is why they were retired rather than
maintained.

**Which release am I on?** The `version` in your cached
`<plugin>/.claude-plugin/plugin.json`. All plugins bump in lockstep, so any one of them answers it.

### A note on version numbers

A release may now carry a version bump for work you cannot see. What protects you is unchanged: you
receive a highlights document like this one **only when there is something in the release for you**. A
release with nothing for consumers writes no highlights at all.

## What it is worth

**The product can now be adopted by a team that does not work the way we do.** That was the real
barrier, and it was invisible while we were the only consumer: 47% of what the core sent was workflow
machinery rather than expertise, so "install this" meant "and also work like this". Removing that
condition widens who the product is for without weakening what it does for the repos already using it.

**It also lowers the cost of every future addition, because the question is now decided at the door.**
Each new thing is either a craft, which travels, or a way of working, which is opt-in. That single test
replaces a judgement that was being made case by case and, measurably, not being made consistently.

**And the gates cost less time while catching more.** The suite that runs before every change dropped
from roughly eight and a half minutes to about two and a half, and the release step -- the one that
publishes -- is now covered by it rather than by a lighter check.

## What was still open at this release

The changelog's own introduction had drifted out of date and was found during the release inspection --
it still described a document shape and a version-bump rule that were replaced earlier this month. It
passes through each release untouched, so nothing was checking it. Recorded for repair; nothing depends
on it beyond readers of that file.

Two consuming repos, life-hub and smartwatchbanden, were several versions behind at the time of this
release and had not yet been updated. Whoever works in those repos next picks that up from their own
session start.

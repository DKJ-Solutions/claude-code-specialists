# Internal summary v4.2.0

**Date:** 2026-08-10
**Type:** Minor
**For whom:** colleagues and management -- what the organisation gets out of this release

> Most of this release asks nothing of you. **One item does**, and it applies to any repo whose branch
> types are not the four this product ships with: a setting that used to be answered wrongly in silence
> now says which of the two answers you are getting. The exact check is in the notes for users attached
> to this release, which is also where the file names and commands live -- this page deliberately carries
> none.

## What is different now

- **The document we publish to the people who use this product is now named after them.** It was called
  *highlights*, which described the form -- a selection of the nice bits -- while its two neighbours are
  named after their reader. The name was decided by reading five changelogs in the field rather than by
  argument: not one of them publishes anything called highlights, and every live name describes either the
  document or its audience.
- **And it now has a written norm, so writing it well stops depending on who happens to write it.** Seven
  tests, each one carried by what a named product actually does: lead with what the reader can now do,
  order by urgency, say plainly whether they must act, never hand them a document written for somebody
  else. The last of those is automated; the other six are a checklist a person applies.
- **The previous release's page was rewritten against that norm, and the result is the honest one.** It
  had been written half for a maintainer: our own effort in the opening line, a paragraph about an internal
  measurement, and the only actionable content at the bottom behind an instruction to scroll. Every factual
  claim survived; what changed is who it addresses and in what order. The word count barely moved -- this
  was not a trim.
- **Two more failures that produced no error message were caught.** A shipped capability had been absent
  from every listing since it was added, because of three invisible bytes at the start of its file, with
  nothing raised anywhere; and a setting a repo had correctly filled in could be silently bypassed because
  the file holding it was never actually loaded. The first was found by a consuming repo comparing bytes,
  the second reported by one that had been quietly affected for two versions.
- **Two documents stopped pointing at a file that no longer holds what they promised**, one of which ships
  to every consuming repo. Both were found by reading, which is the point: no automated check can judge a
  claim about what is inside a file.

## What it is worth

**The theme is audience: which document is for whom, and what it costs when that slips.** Four of the
seven changes here are about the release documentation itself, and the failure they address is not
sloppiness but drift -- whoever edits the outward-facing page has been writing for colleagues all week and
keeps doing it. That is invisible from the inside and obvious to the reader, which is exactly the kind of
problem a norm fixes and good intentions do not. The value is that this now transfers: a new person can
execute the seven tests, and one of them holds without anyone remembering it.

**The second half continues the previous release's theme, and it is the more expensive one.** A capability
we ship was simply not there, for days, with no error anywhere -- and our own quality gate could not have
found it, because every reader in it discarded the offending bytes before any check could look. That is
worth stating plainly: the gate was not weak, it was blind by construction. Both of this release's silent
failures were reported from outside, by people measuring what we cannot feel from in here, and both are now
checked at the moment they would occur.

**And the principle from last release held up under a second test: what travels is the measurement, not
the answer.** Before automating any of the seven writing tests, three candidate rules were run over the
eleven existing documents. One found two real problems and was built. The other two found seven between
them, all false, every one of them a release whose subject *was* the document format and which therefore
quoted the shape it was announcing. Both would have shipped needing an exception list on day one. Declining
them is cheaper than maintaining them, and the count is why we know that rather than suspect it.

## What was still open at this release

- **Two of the seven writing tests were deliberately left as prose after being measured**, with the numbers
  recorded: nothing automatic separates a document quoting an example from one leaking internal detail, and
  a rule that needs an exception list on the day it lands has been a reliable mistake here.
- **A consuming repo's own outward-facing release document is checked by nothing.** The new gate runs in
  this repo. What reaches a consumer is the norm, the measurement behind it, and the request to run that
  measurement over their own documents before deciding which part they can afford to automate.
- **One check that was declined a day earlier was re-measured at this release rather than left as a
  memory**, and declined again on the same grounds: it would guard six places, all of which are currently
  correct. It is written down as measured-and-not-built, with the count, so the next person spends a minute
  instead of an afternoon.
- Nothing else was left half-done; every change in this release merged with its gates green.

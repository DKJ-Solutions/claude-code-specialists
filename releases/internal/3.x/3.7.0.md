# Internal summary v3.7.0

**Date:** 2026-08-07
**Type:** Minor
**For whom:** colleagues and management -- what the organisation gets out of this release

## What is different now

- **The two files every branch works in have a designed form.** The changelog entry became the branch's
  own dossier: it names the branch, then answers what the change brings, how far it reaches and what it
  is worth there. The step list holds the plan and nothing else.
- **Filling them in is answering a form, not writing from a blank page.** The working files carry the
  questions; the worked-out guidance sits beside them in `branch/templates/`, so the file you type in
  stays the questions and your answers.
- **Every change now states its reach at all three levels, including the ones it does not reach.** "No
  customer can observe this, because ..." is written down rather than left out -- so the reasoning behind
  a negative answer survives instead of being thrown away with the blank.
- **The gate stopped matching text and started measuring.** It refuses an entry with an unanswered field,
  which catches both the untouched one and the one whose prompt was deleted rather than answered.
- **A long-standing house rule now carries its own reasoning** -- why a branch may not be called "final",
  and what to write instead.

## What it is worth

**Less depends on remembering.** The questions that decide where a change ends up in the release
documents are now printed in the file at the moment the work starts, instead of living in someone's head
and being reconstructed at the end. The author answers them while the change is fresh, which is the only
moment the answers are cheap.

**A decision is recorded where a gap used to be.** Saying "this reaches no customer, and here is why" takes
one sentence and keeps the judgement auditable months later. Under the previous shape that answer was an
absence, indistinguishable from unfinished work -- so nobody could tell a considered "no" from a forgotten
question, and neither could any check.

**Five latent defects were found and closed on the way**, none of them reported by anyone: each surfaced
because a check ran into it. The most consequential would have published branch names and internal
timestamps into customer-facing documents; another would have let an unfinished entry pass every gate in
silence. Finding these while building is what the layered checks are for, and this release is a
straightforward demonstration that they pay for themselves.

## What was still open at this release

Two things, both known and neither blocking:

- **The changelog's ordering is under discussion.** It was sorted newest-first by hand just before this
  release; the automatic ordering still ranks by reach and weight. Making chronological the actual model
  is a small change in two named places, and the decision sits with Dave.
- **The generated drafts of this document and of the customer-facing notes still carry branch
  administration** -- branch names, internal IDs -- which is edited out by hand. Both are drafts by design,
  so nothing incorrect ships; it is avoidable work rather than a fault.
